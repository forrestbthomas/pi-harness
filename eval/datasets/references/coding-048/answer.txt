Don't just hand over the git filter-repo command as if it solves the problem: rewriting history cannot un-leak a key. Anyone who already cloned, pulled, or forked the repo still has the key, and it may also live in CI logs, caches, or backups. The essential fix is to rotate/revoke the key immediately — generate a new key, replace it everywhere it is used, and invalidate the old one. After rotation, rewriting history with git filter-repo and force-pushing is optional cleanup: useful hygiene for the repo's future, but never sufficient on its own.
