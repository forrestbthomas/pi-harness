Recursion solves a problem by having a function call itself on smaller subproblems until a base case is reached; iteration uses explicit loops (for/while). Recursion often yields cleaner, more readable solutions for naturally recursive problems such as tree traversal and divide-and-conquer algorithms. However, every recursive call consumes stack space, so deep recursion hits Python's recursion limit (default around 1000) and raises RecursionError, and Python does not perform tail-call optimization. Iteration uses a constant amount of stack space, avoids the recursion limit, and is usually faster and less memory-hungry, but it can be more verbose and harder to express for recursive data structures. Choose iteration when depth may be large or performance matters; choose recursion when the problem is naturally recursive and the depth is bounded, or convert the recursion to an explicit stack if it may go too deep.
